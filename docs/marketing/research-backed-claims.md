# Research-backed marketing claims — ready to use

Pull-and-paste claims for landing pages, pricing page, ads, social posts, or a pitch deck. Each one is grounded in real research (see [sla-theory-app-mapping.md](../research/sla-theory-app-mapping.md) for the underlying citations if anyone ever asks) but written as consumer copy — no jargon, no academic names in the claim itself.

**Rule of thumb:** say "research shows" or "the science of learning shows," never name a specific paper/author in the ad copy. Never promise a specific score increase — you have no outcome data yet. Claim the *method* is research-backed, not the *result*.

## Do's and don'ts

**Do:**
- Say "research shows" / "studies show" / "backed by learning science."
- Claim the *method* is proven (spaced review, active recall, personalized pacing, real test format, immediate feedback).
- Use these as supporting bullets near a price or CTA — they reduce doubt right where someone is deciding.

**Don't:**
- Don't promise a specific score increase ("+15 points guaranteed") — you don't have outcome data to back that yet.
- Don't name specific researchers/papers in ad copy or landing pages (fine for a footnote/About-page link, not for a headline).
- Don't say "clinically proven," "certified by researchers," or "endorsed by [university/Duolingo]" — none of that is true; "backed by research" is the accurate, defensible framing.
- Don't use more than 3-4 of these per page — stacking too many dilutes rather than builds trust.
