How to use the key

Where a question has more than one natural answer, alternatives are shown after a slash. For translation and speaking tasks, your wording may differ — check that the chunk is right, not that every word matches.

Sources & Further Reading

แหล่งอ้างอิง — ทุกตัวเลขในเล่มนี้มาจากงานวิจัยจริง

Nothing in this book was chosen by instinct. Every list was filtered through corpus frequency data, and every claim about Thai learners comes from published error analysis.

Vocabulary size and CEFR levels

SourceWhat it gave this book
Milton, J. & Alexiou, T. — Vocabulary size and the CEFR B1 learners command roughly 2,500–3,000 word families, typically ~2,750 — stable across languages.
Oxford University Press — The Oxford 3000 by CEFR level (American English) Counted directly: A1 = 902, A2 = 869, B1 = 809 headwords. 2,580 to cover B1; the A2→B1 step is 809 new words.
Cambridge Assessment English — B1 Preliminary Vocabulary List (Aug 2025) Counted directly: the official B1 exam list contains only 94 phrasal verbs and 45 British/American variant pairs.

Phrasal verbs

SourceWhat it gave this book
Garnier, M. & Schmitt, N. (2015) — The PHaVE List, Language Teaching Research Built on COCA (American English): 150 phrasal verbs cover 75%+ of all occurrences, and two senses cover 75%+ of each verb. This is why the book teaches two senses, never five.
Gardner, D. & Davies, M. (2007) — Pointing Out Frequent Phrasal Verbs, TESOL Quarterly 100 phrasal verbs = 51.4% of all use; 25 = nearly a third; 20 verbs + 8 particles = 50.4%. This is the basis of Chapter 1.
Liu, D. (2011) — The Most Frequently Used English Phrasal Verbs in American and British English, TESOL Quarterly Cross-variety comparison behind Chapter 7’s American/British swap list.

Collocations and multi-word items

SourceWhat it gave this book
Shin, D. & Nation, P. (2008) — Beyond single words, ELT Journal The first 100 pivot words alone yield 2,052 collocations — proof that a textbook must choose a principle rather than try to be complete.
Martinez, R. & Schmitt, N. (2012) — A Phrasal Expressions List, Applied Linguistics The 505 most frequent non-transparent multi-word expressions — the filter that removed “raining cats and dogs” from Part Two.

Thai learners specifically

SourceWhat it gave this book
Boonraksa, T. & Naisena, S. (2022) — A Study on English Collocation Errors of Thai EFL Students L1 transfer is the number-one error source. Adjective + noun was the worst lexical pattern at 21.31% of violations.
Yumanee, C. — Analysis of Collocational Errors of Thai EFL Students, LEARN Journal Highest error counts fall on noun + preposition and adjective + preposition, and Thai students most often omit a required preposition. This is the entire rationale for Part Three.

Learning and retention

SourceWhat it gave this book
Research on spaced vs. massed vocabulary learning (ERIC review; classroom studies) Spaced review produced about three times the retention of cramming — the basis of the “come back in three days” instruction and the recycling design.
A note for teachers

Every item in this book carries five hidden tags — form, frequency tier, theme, Thai-trap type, and register — so any page can be regenerated as a worksheet for a specific weakness: all Tier-1 preposition traps, or every American/British pair, or spoken chunks only.
